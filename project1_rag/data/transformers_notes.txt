## 1. Introduction to Transformers

- Introduced in the 2017 paper "Attention Is All You Need" by Vaswani et al.

- Replaced Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and LSTMs for sequence-to-sequence tasks.

- Core innovation: Completely relies on **self-attention mechanisms** to compute representations of input and output without using sequence-aligned RNNs or convolutions.

- Highly parallelizable, making it suitable for training on massive modern hardware (GPUs/TPUs).



---



## 2. Core Components of the Transformer Architecture



### A. Tokenization & Embeddings

- **Tokenization:** Breaking raw text into subwords (e.g., using Byte-Pair Encoding or WordPiece) so models can handle unseen words gracefully.

- **Input Embeddings:** Converting token IDs into dense vectors of dimension $d_{\text{model}}$ (e.g., 768, 4096).

- **Positional Encodings:** Because Transformers process all tokens simultaneously (lacking sequential recurrence), positional encodings (sinusoidal or learned) are added to token embeddings to preserve word order information.



### B. Scaled Dot-Product Attention

- The fundamental building block of attention.

- Calculated using Queries ($Q$), Keys ($K$), and Values ($V$):

  $$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$

- **$d_k$:** Scaling factor (dimension of the keys) to prevent gradients from vanishing or exploding in the softmax function for large dimensions.



### C. Multi-Head Attention

- Instead of performing a single attention function, the queries, keys, and values are projected $h$ times with different, learned linear projections.

- Allows the model to jointly attend to information from different representation subspaces at different positions.



### D. Encoder-Decoder Structure (Original Architecture)

- **Encoder:** Maps an input sequence of symbol representations to a sequence of continuous representations. Composed of alternating multi-head self-attention and position-wise fully connected feed-forward layers.

- **Decoder:** Generates an output sequence one element at a time, utilizing masked self-attention (to prevent looking into future tokens) and encoder-decoder cross-attention.

## 3. Transformer Variants & Their Role in RAG



### A. Encoder-Only Models (e.g., BERT, RoBERTa)

- **Characteristics:** Bidirectional context, trained via Masked Language Modeling (MLM).

- **RAG Use Case:** Excellent for **Embeddings** and **Cross-Encoders (Rerankers)**. Used to encode documents and queries into dense vector spaces for semantic vector search (e.g., in vector databases like FAISS, Chroma, Pinecone).



### B. Decoder-Only Models (e.g., GPT-4, Llama 3, Mistral)

- **Characteristics:** Autoregressive, unidirectional, trained on Causal Language Modeling (CLM).

- **RAG Use Case:** Acting as the **Generator** component in RAG. They take the retrieved context passages along with the user prompt to synthesize a coherent, grounded final response.
